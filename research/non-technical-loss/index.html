Finding electricity theft by making the grid balance its own books

Utilities lose roughly $100bn a year to non-technical losses, yet untargeted inspection is itself loss-making — which is why it rarely happens. This project reframes theft detection as an inspection-budget problem and reports what a detector actually delivers under realistic, partially-labelled conditions.

The constraint the model enforces
Σ metered kWh+ technical loss+ stolen kWh= transformer head reading

Every low-voltage feeder is a closed accounting system. Prior work treats the transformer reading as one more input feature; here it becomes a physical constraint inside the loss function, so the network must attribute the unexplained residual to specific meters — and can therefore report estimated stolen kilowatt-hours, not just a probability.

Only 48 of the 1,158 meters carried confirmed labels while true prevalence was 13%, so two thirds of thieves were unlabelled. Training therefore used positive-unlabelled learning, and a deliberately mis-set class prior was found to collapse training after six epochs. Diagnosing that, characterising it with a prior sweep, and fixing it produced the result above. Evaluating against confirmed labels alone understates true performance by a measured 1.48×, which makes every real-world figure here a lower bound.

Energy-preserving theft — where a customer shifts consumption without reducing the total — defeats every configuration including this one, because it violates no conservation law. Flag rates otherwise track each region's real prevalence, so the model does not over-target; but context features widen the recall gap between regions, a trade-off surfaced here rather than left unmeasured.

Methods & scope
  • TemporalDilated convolution with self-attention over daily consumption
  • SpatialMasked graph attention over the true meter-to-transformer topology
  • PhysicsConservation loss plus a softplus head estimating stolen kWh per meter
  • LabelsNon-negative positive-unlabelled learning with a prior sweep
  • PretrainingMasked-span reconstruction on unlabelled series
  • AugmentationLiterature attack functions plus a conditional VAE over theft masks
  • OutputTemperature-calibrated ranking by probability × estimated stolen energy
  • Energy-preserving theft remains largely undetected — by this model and by every published alternative.
  • Graph and physics contributions are not statistically separable on aggregate metrics at this network scale.
  • Validation is a physics-based simulator plus a hidden-truth rehearsal; a prospective pilot with a randomised control arm is the outstanding step.
  • Out of scope by design: adversarial evasion, privacy-preserving training, appliance-level disaggregation, streaming inference, learned inspection policies.
