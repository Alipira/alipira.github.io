Finding electricity theft by making the grid balance its own books

Utilities can inspect only a small fraction of customers each year, so what matters is how many thieves, and how much stolen energy, sit at the top of the inspection list. This project treats theft detection as ranking under a fixed inspection budget with partial labels, and reports what a detector delivers on a simulated network and in a deployment rehearsal with hidden truth.

The constraint the model enforces
Σ metered kWh+ technical loss+ stolen kWh= transformer head reading

Every low-voltage feeder is a closed accounting system. Earlier work uses the transformer reading as a screening gate or as one more input feature; here the balance enters the training objective, and a conservation head estimates unbilled kilowatt-hours per meter, not just a probability. The constraint fixes how much energy a feeder is missing, never which meter took it: attribution within a feeder comes from the learned branches.

A per-customer CNN, given each meter's reported series only, is at least as accurate as the full model. The comparison is not controlled: the CNN splits customers, so meters from one feeder land in both training and test, while PI-STGAT holds out whole feeders; and the CNN trains with augmentation matching six of the eight test attacks. The augmented PI-STGAT variant scores higher, but under a different split. A same-split, same-augmentation comparison is the next step; an earlier claim that the architecture recovers a large “cost of network realism” is withdrawn.

Five seeds give little power; 5/5 is the strongest pattern five seeds can show, not a significance test.

Static attributes help on every seed, and still do when meter age and alarm counts are redrawn independently of theft. Feeder attention is not separable from noise. The physics branch lowers AUPR slightly on four of five seeds, as expected: the conservation term fixes feeder totals, not the within-feeder ranking that AUPR measures. Its value is the stolen-energy estimate. A positional code in the temporal encoder and SSL with augmentation each raise AUPR on every seed by about 0.035.

Share of thieves in the top of each ranking · test feeders of five exports pooled (901 meters, 142 thieves) · range over five PU priors, bar drawn at the lowest

Each export carries the defects a utility file would: gaps, blank rows, late joiners, dead and unmapped meters, wrong transformer links, unmetered load and head noise. Only 30% of thieves are labelled, so the confirmed rate (3–4%) is far below true prevalence (10–15%). Setting the PU prior to that confirmed rate puts training in the regime where the objective stops rewarding detection: validation ROC-AUC fell below 0.5 in 22% of epochs on average, and never at a prior of 0.13 or above. Selecting the best validation epoch still recovered usable rankings, and no prior was consistently best. Evaluating against confirmed labels alone understated true AUPR by 3.5× on average (predicted 3.7×, range 1.2–8.3), so real-world confirmed-only figures should be read as lower bounds.

Reversed consumption keeps the same total and the same distribution, so it is invisible to the conservation constraint and to every permutation-invariant feature. The analysis predicted that a calendar reference would help, and adding a positional code raised reversal recall to 0.48 (random ranking gives 0.10). Flag rates track each region's prevalence. Recall is balanced for the temporal-only model (0.80 vs 0.80) and becomes unbalanced once static attributes enter; removing the region input narrows the gap only from 0.16 to 0.13, while the two strongest variants narrow it by lifting low-region recall (0.85 vs 0.93, 0.87 vs 0.94).

Methods & scope
  • TemporalDilated convolution with self-attention over daily consumption; optional positional code
  • SpatialAttention among meters that share a transformer
  • PhysicsConservation loss plus a head estimating unbilled kWh per meter
  • LabelsNon-negative positive-unlabelled learning, with an analysis and sweep of the class prior
  • PretrainingMasked-span reconstruction on unlabelled series
  • AugmentationLiterature attack functions plus a conditional VAE over theft masks
  • OutputTemperature-calibrated ranking by probability × estimated stolen energy
  • No advantage over a well-trained per-customer CNN is established; a same-split comparison is pending.
  • Feeder attention and the physics branch give no discrimination gain at this network scale.
  • Energy-preserving reversal stays the hardest attack (0.28–0.48 recall@10%).
  • The simulator was written by the author and no real utility data has been used; a prospective pilot with a randomised inspection arm is the outstanding step.
  • Out of scope by design: adversarial evasion, privacy-preserving training, appliance-level disaggregation, streaming inference, learned inspection policies.
